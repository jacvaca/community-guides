This repository is a living library of **community how‑to guides** for people who build, maintain, and care for online spaces.

## What this repo is

- A collection of practical guides, templates, and playbooks for community work.
- Sourced from:
  - Aggregated insights from multiple community professionals.
  - Direct contributions and write-ups from operators in the field.
- Focused on real-world execution: what to do on Monday morning, not just theory.

## Why it exists

The work of community professionals is often fragmented, under-documented, and learned the hard way in DMs and buried threads. This repo aims to:

- **Simplify** community operations with clear, reusable guides.
- **Unify** shared knowledge so community pros aren’t reinventing the wheel alone.
- **Level up** industry standards for:
  - How we design and run communities.
  - How we care for the humans who gather online.
  - How we measure impact beyond vanity metrics.

## How to use this repo

- Copy, adapt, and remix resources for your own community context.
- Use as a reference for:
  - Training new community managers.
  - Auditing and upgrading existing community practices.
  - Aligning your team on building resilient online communities.

## Contributions

This is intentionally collaborative.

- If you’re a community professional with a battle-tested tactic, framework, or template:
  - Open a PR with a new guide.
  - Or improve clarity, examples, and structure in existing ones.
- Please:
  - Keep contributions practical and specific.
  - Anonymize sensitive details if you’re sharing real incidents.
  - Avoid sharing proprietary or private company information.

## Who this is for

- Community managers, heads of community, and community-adjacent roles.
- Founders and operators spinning up or scaling communities.
- Anyone responsible for the health, safety, and experience of people gathering online.

If this repo helps you ship a better community experience, prevents burnout, or gives you language for advocating for the craft internally, then it’s doing its job.
